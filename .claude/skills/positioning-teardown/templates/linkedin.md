## Mode

**Teardown mode** (X hook mode #5, adapted for LinkedIn).

Structure: Public benchmark + I tore it down + here's what's broken + what to do about it.

This is a wedge for the positioning + web design messaging service. The post should make ICP founders self-identify ("oh, my page does that too") and DM. Not lead-magnet bait.

---

## Headline / hook (line 1, the only one above the "see more" fold)

Pick one of these patterns. Plug in real numbers and the company name.

**Pattern 1: Receipt one-liner**
> {{COMPANY}}'s homepage tells me they have {{X}} features. It doesn't tell me which one solves my problem.

**Pattern 2: The "what does it do" failure**
> Spent 3 minutes on {{COMPANY}}.com. Still can't tell you what they do in one sentence.

**Pattern 3: The platitude count**
> {{COMPANY}}'s homepage uses "platform" {{N}} times and "modern teams" {{M}} times. Their actual buyer is a {{ICP}} who doesn't care about either.

**Pattern 4: The buyer awareness mismatch**
> {{COMPANY}}'s site is written for a buyer who already knows {{X}}. Their actual traffic shows up at {{Y}} and bounces.

**Pattern 5: The Founder Vision Mode call-out**
> {{COMPANY}} is selling a platform vision. Their buyer wants to know if they can replace their {{TOOL}} by Friday. The site can't answer either question.

Pick the pattern that fits the actual finding. Don't shoehorn.

## Voice rules to enforce (cross-check before posting)

Before publishing, run the post through `ijas-voice`. Specifically:

- [ ] Zero em dashes
- [ ] Specific numbers in at least three places (not adjectives)
- [ ] Named company in headline or first paragraph
- [ ] First person, past tense for the build ("I tore down")
- [ ] No "thoughts?" / "what do you think?" / "let me know"
- [ ] No fake scarcity, no "comment X for the link"
- [ ] No "in today's fast-paced world" / "AI is transforming" / "buckle up"
- [ ] Line breaks every 1-2 sentences for LinkedIn skim
- [ ] CTA is "DM if you want X" or just nothing. Not lead-magnet bait.
- [ ] One idea. If two findings, split into two posts.

## Two delivery modes

### Mode A: Named company (the wedge play)

Names {{COMPANY}} explicitly. Highest engagement, highest risk. Use when:
- Company is well-known enough that the post lands without context
- The teardown is fair (not punitive. Show what's working too)
- The user is OK with the risk that the company sees it

### Mode B: Anonymized

"A Series A startup in {{CATEGORY}} I audited last week..." Lower engagement, lower risk. Use when:
- The actual prospect is in the user's outbound list
- The company isn't well-known enough to drive recognition
- The user prefers to stay diplomatic

**Default:** Named, unless the user flags otherwise.

---

## The "soft close" line

LinkedIn teardowns convert when they end with a line that makes the reader self-identify. Something like:

- "If your hero starts with the word 'platform', this is you."
- "If your homepage tells me 'who you are' before 'what you do', this is you."
- "If your CTA is 'Get Started' and your buyer has never heard of you, this is you."

Pick the one that matches the lead finding. One line. Then the DM line, then nothing.

---

## What not to do

- Don't write a generic "5 lessons from a positioning teardown" post. The specificity is the whole product.
- Don't post the rewrite copy as the headline. The teardown is the hook; the rewrite is what they DM for.
- Don't soften the call-outs. The voice rules say "named tool, named company, specific number." Soft = no engagement.
- Don't add a CTA that asks for likes or comments. Nothing kills reach faster on a sharp post than asking.
