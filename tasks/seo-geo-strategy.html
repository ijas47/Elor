Owning "custom chandelier" across Kerala and India

A search strategy for two brands, one business: what each domain should rank for, how to reach every Kerala city without a store in it, and what actually gets you cited by AI answer engines.

19 August 2026 Horizon: 6 months Status: proposal

01The starting position

elorlighting.com went live this month. Google Search Console is verified and returning zero impressions, which is exactly what a one-week-old domain should return. The technical foundation is unusually good for a site this young — canonical domain fixed, security headers, BreadcrumbList/Article/FAQPage/HowTo/LightingStore schema, an llms.txt, real lastmod dates, an IndexNow key, and eleven indexable pages including five content guides.

What the site does not have is any reason for Google to trust it yet: no named humans, no reviews, no third-party citations, no backlinks, and no presence on the directories that already rank for the queries you want. That gap — not the code — is the binding constraint for the next 90 days.

Two things change the picture from here. Celestial Lights is about to launch as an ecommerce site, which splits the business across two domains. And the ambition is pan-India delivery plus full Kerala coverage from a three-store physical footprint. Both are solvable. Both are easy to get wrong in ways that are expensive to undo later, which is why the architecture decisions in section 02 come before any content work.

02Two brands, one entity, zero overlap

The single most consequential decision in this plan is which domain owns which query. Get it wrong and the two sites compete with each other for the same terms, splitting link equity and confusing Google about which page to rank — the classic sister-brand failure.

The split follows the actual business logic already encoded in lib/site.ts: Elor is the consulting and custom-fabrication arm, Celestial Lights is the retail brand and the experience-store estate.

The hard rule

Elor never creates a page per product. Celestial never creates a page per city. Elor's /collections stays a curated gallery of 23 pieces with no individual product URLs — every piece links out to its Celestial product page. That is not a compromise; it is the thing that stops the two domains cannibalising each other, and it means Elor's gallery page accumulates all the link equity instead of spreading it across 23 thin pages.

The trade-off you are accepting

Two domains split authority. A subfolder — elorlighting.com/shop — would consolidate everything into one growing domain and is what I would recommend if Shopify made it easy. It doesn't: subfolder hosting requires a reverse proxy and is a Shopify Plus feature. So two domains is the pragmatic answer, and the mitigations below are not optional extras — they are what buys back most of the lost authority.

Cross-domain mitigations — do all five

  1. Mutual sameAs in Organization schema. Elor's Organization declares Celestial in sameAs and parentOrganization; Celestial declares Elor in sameAs and subOrganization. This tells Google they are one entity, not two unrelated sites.
  2. Identical NAP everywhere. Same phone (+91 99956 19470), same email pattern, same store addresses, character-for-character, on both domains and every directory listing. Inconsistent NAP is the most common local-SEO self-inflicted wound.
  3. Prominent editorial cross-links. Not a footer link. Elor's collections page links into Celestial product pages; Celestial's product pages link back to Elor for "want this customised?" and "see it in a store". Both directions, in the content body.
  4. Shared social profiles listed in both sameAs arrays.
  5. One Google Business Profile set, not two. Three profiles for three stores, each naming its trading brand correctly (Kannur and Kozhikode as Celestial Lights, Kochi as Elor Lighting), all linking to the right domain.

03Pan-India coverage without doorway pages

You asked for city pages "buried somewhere" so they pull traffic. I want to flag one thing plainly, then give you the version that gets the same traffic without the risk.

Why "buried" is both risky and self-defeating

A set of near-identical location pages, hidden from navigation, existing only to catch search traffic is the textbook definition of a doorway page — a named, actioned violation in Google's spam policies. The penalty is usually sitewide, not page-level, so it would take down the pages that are actually working.

It also doesn't work mechanically. Pages with no internal links get crawled rarely, accumulate no PageRank, and are the first candidates for "Crawled — currently not indexed". Burying a page is the most reliable way to stop it ranking.

The legitimate structure that delivers what you actually want — pan-India and full-Kerala reach, kept out of the main navigation so it doesn't clutter the brand — has three parts.

1. Discoverable hub, not main nav

A single index page at /lighting listing every city you serve, linked from the footer and from each store page. That is "buried" in the sense you meant — a customer browsing the brand never trips over it — while still being one click from a crawlable page. Hub-and-spoke, not orphaned.

2. Two page types, honestly distinguished

This is what separates a legitimate service-area page from a doorway page, and it is not a subtle distinction to Google:

Store cities (3)Service-area cities (all others)
URL/stores/kannur/lighting/thrissur
Claims"Visit our Kannur store""We deliver and install in Thrissur from our Kochi store"
SchemaLightingStore with address + geoService with areaServed — no address, no LocalBusiness
Google Business ProfileYes, one eachNo
Content coreStore, staff, hours, directions, local projectsDelivery logistics, material guidance for that location, local project types

Publishing a fake address or LocalBusiness schema for a city you have no premises in is the line between aggressive and fraudulent. We stay well behind it — and the honest framing ("installed in Thrissur, dispatched from Kochi, 2 hours away") converts better anyway, because it answers the question the searcher actually has.

3. Genuine differentiation per page

Thin content is what kills location pages, and swapping a city name into a template is thin. Each service-area page is generated from a real per-city data record, so the differences are factual rather than cosmetic:

  • Nearest store, real distance and drive time — and what that means for site visits and installation scheduling
  • Coastal or inland — Kerala's coastal belt is salt-laden and humid, which genuinely rules out untreated brass, iron and mild steel in favour of crystal, glass, stainless and powder-coated finishes. Real, useful, and different for Wayanad than for Alappuzha.
  • Dominant building typology — Kochi apartments and villas, Wayanad and Idukki hill properties with high ceilings and condensation, Palakkad and Thrissur traditional courtyard homes, Kozhikode commercial and hospitality
  • Real projects delivered there — the strongest differentiator, and the one I need data from you for
  • City-specific FAQs — delivery window, whether a site visit is free at that distance, installation lead time
  • Named localities within each city, which gives the page natural entity richness without keyword stuffing

Rough target: 600–900 words of genuinely unique content per page. Below that, expect indexation problems.

05Content that nobody else in India owns

Ranking for "chandelier Kochi" against White Teak and IndiaMART is a grind. Ranking for questions nobody has answered properly is not. These are queries where Indian buyers are demonstrably underserved because the entire content corpus is Western, and where a well-structured answer gets pulled straight into AI answers.

WedgeWhy it is uncontestedFormat
Chandelier + ceiling fan Nearly every Indian room has a ceiling fan. Almost no Western lighting content addresses the clearance, offset and airflow problem. Highest-value gap on this list. Guide with a clearance table and diagrams
False ceiling load limits Gypsum and POP false ceilings are standard in Indian construction and cannot carry a 40 kg fixture — it needs an anchor into the RCC slab. Buyers find this out too late, contractors get it wrong. Guide with weight thresholds by ceiling type
Sizing calculator Free interactive tools earn links and get cited by AI as a resource. Room dimensions → diameter, ceiling height → drop, plus the Indian layer nobody adds: fan clearance and ceiling-load check. Client-side calculator, no backend
Coastal corrosion Kerala's coast destroys untreated metal finishes. Real, specific, verifiable, and it makes every city page more useful. Guide + reused across city pages
Building in Kerala from the Gulf Kerala's residential construction is heavily NRI-funded. Remote consultation, video handover, family site visits, shipping timelines. The Living Influence proves the demand exists; nobody owns the Kerala-side version. Guide + a dedicated consultation path
Crystal vs glass vs acrylic Comparison content is ~33% of all AI citations — the single most-cited format. You have zero comparison pages today. Comparison table, one page per pairing
Traditional Kerala homes Lighting a nalukettu courtyard, sloped tiled roofs, exposed rafters. Deeply local, culturally credible, entirely unwritten. Guide, doubles as portfolio

Query fan-out

Google's AI features generate several related queries behind the one the user typed, then synthesise across all of them. That means a cluster of pages covering a whole topic beats one page per keyword. These seven pieces plus the two existing guides form a coherent "custom lighting in India" cluster — they should all interlink, and each should link back to /guides as the hub.

06The AEO layer — getting cited, not just ranked

AI Overviews now appear in roughly 45% of Google searches and cut clicks to websites by up to 58%. Ranking without being cited increasingly means being invisible. The Princeton GEO research ranked which interventions actually move AI citation rates, and Elor's gaps map onto the top four almost exactly.

LeverMeasured liftElor todayFix
Cite sources+40%NoneCite IS standards, BIS wiring norms, manufacturer specs in guides
Add statistics+37%Almost noneCeiling-height thresholds, weight limits, lumens per m², lead times in days, warranty in years, project counts
Add quotations+30%None — no named humans exist on the siteNamed founder and designer, quoted with title, on guides and the About page
Authoritative tone+25%Partial — copy is art-directed, not expert-voicedBylines with credentials, "According to…" framing

The biggest single AEO gap is that no human being is named anywhere on the site

Three of the top four citation levers — quotations, authoritative tone, expert attribution — all require a named person with credentials. An About page with the founder and lead designer, Person schema, and bylines on every guide is the highest-leverage change available, and it is blocked only on you sending me names, roles, years of experience and photos.

Structure for extraction

AI systems extract passages, not pages. Applied to Elor's content:

  • Lead every section with the answer. 40–60 words, self-contained, working without the surrounding context.
  • Headings phrased as questions where the query is a question — "How heavy a chandelier can a false ceiling hold?" outranks "Ceiling Considerations".
  • Tables beat prose for anything comparative. Numbered lists beat paragraphs for anything procedural.
  • Dates on everything. Undated content loses to dated content. The Guides pages already carry "Updated"; extend to service pages when they change materially.

One caution: Google explicitly warns against fragmenting content into AI-bait chunks or writing separate copy "for AI" — that risks the scaled-content-abuse policy. Everything above is just clear writing, which is why it satisfies both Google and the non-Google engines.

Machine-readable layer

  • llms.txt — live already reads from lib/guides.ts, so new guides appear automatically. Extend it to name the service-area cities.
  • /pricing.md — plain-text price bands for AI agents comparing options before a human ever visits. Elor's ready-collection bands are already published in the pricing guide; mirroring them as a parseable file costs nothing.
  • AI crawlers — clear robots.ts allows all, including GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended. Keep it that way.
  • IndexNow — key file is live, but URLs are not yet submitted on publish. Wire the submission.

Presence — where AI actually looks

Brands are 6.5× more likely to be cited via third-party sources than via their own domain. This is the part that cannot be coded, and it is currently at zero:

  • Google Business Profile ×3 — the single highest-value action in this entire document. Local pack visibility for "chandelier Kannur" comes from GBP, not from schema. Nothing on the site substitutes for it.
  • IndiaMART, Justdial, Dial4Trade, Sulekha — they already rank for your city queries. Being listed is the fastest route into those results and into AI answers.
  • Houzz India — where architects and interior designers actually search, and your commercial buyer lives there
  • YouTube — frequently cited by AI Overviews. The showroom video is already shot and currently linked nowhere.
  • Reddit — r/Kerala, r/india, r/InteriorDesign. Genuine participation only; ~1.8% of all ChatGPT citations come from Reddit.
  • Architect and builder partnerships — the most durable backlinks in this industry come from project credits

08Celestial Lights — decisions to make before launch

These are cheap now and expensive after launch. Shopify's defaults will actively work against you on several of them.

  • Canonical URL discipline. Shopify serves every product at both /products/x and /collections/y/products/x. Canonicalise to /products/x from day one or you launch with duplicate content across the entire catalogue.
  • Pick www or apex and enforce it with redirects — the exact bug that took a week to fix on Elor.
  • Full Product schema — offers, price, priceCurrency: INR, availability, sku. Non-negotiable for AI shopping surfaces.
  • Google Merchant Center feed from launch. Google explicitly names Merchant Center as a source for product visibility in AI Search.
  • Review collection from the first order. AggregateRating is worthless without real reviews, and real reviews take months to accumulate. Start on day one. Never fabricate these — it is a manual-action risk and AI engines cross-check.
  • Cross-domain schema and links wired at launch, not retrofitted.
  • Flip site.shopLive to true and update site.shopUrl — a single value in lib/site.ts updates every handoff across the Elor site.
  • Do not link the staging URL publicly until the real domain is live, and noindex the Vercel preview.
