# Elor social — how a lighting plan is built

Source page: https://www.elorlighting.com/guides/how-a-lighting-plan-is-built
Sister page: https://www.elorlighting.com/guides/colour-of-light

www.lightflux.in is a one-page lighting-design studio (Delhi NCR). Their teaching is the method: what deserves light, layers, wall wash, ceiling detail, scenes. These posts teach that method in Elor’s words, for Kerala houses, and they send people to Elor’s guide. Film them in the Kannur, Kochi, or Kozhikode centre. Do not repost Lightflux photographs, drawings, or sentences. Do not quote their scene percentages.

Pair with `tasks/social/colour-of-light.md`. Plan posts on Monday. Number posts (Kelvin, CRI, beam) on Thursday. Two a week is enough.

---

## 1. Reel — the ceiling is last (20 sec)

Walk a showroom or a site. Start on the sofa or the table. End on the ceiling.

Text, in order:
What should be seen.
Then the furniture.
Then the wall.
The ceiling hides the fittings.

Spoken:
“A lighting plan is a list of what the room needs you to notice. The downlights are how we hide the work. They are not the design.”

Caption:
We draw the sofa, the table, and the wall before we draw a single point. If your ceiling is still open, send the floor plan.

---

## 2. Carousel — five layers

Slide 1. Five layers. Five switches.
Slide 2. Ambient. Enough to cross the room.
Slide 3. Wall and art. Vertical brightness. This is what makes a room feel deep.
Slide 4. Task. The worktop, the desk, the mirror. Off when the work stops.
Slide 5. Concealed. Cove and pelmet. Depth, not a hotspot.
Slide 6. The chandelier. The object in the room. It does not replace the other four.

Caption:
One switch for all of this gives you a bright ceiling and a flat room. The guide on the site is the order we mark them in.

---

## 3. Reel — scallops (15 sec)

If a centre wall shows scallops from downlights, film that wall, then a washed wall in the same centre.

Text:
This is a downlight too close to the wall.
This is a wall wash.

Spoken:
“Those arcs are the beam hitting the wall from the wrong setback. A wash sits back, usually somewhere around 600 to 900 millimetres on a home ceiling, and the spacing follows. We check it on the section.”

Caption:
A row of bright arcs on a wall is a setback problem. It is fixed on the drawing, before the gypsum closes.

---

## 4. Carousel — three evenings

Slide 1. Same fittings. Three evenings.
Slide 2. Quiet. Ambient low. Chandelier low. A little light left on the wall.
Slide 3. Guests. Ambient up. The piece on. Task off.
Slide 4. Art, or the courtyard. Wall light full. Ambient down, so the wall leads.
Slide 5. If every layer shares one dimmer, you only have brighter and darker.

Caption:
Scenes are why the circuits are separate. We set the levels in the room once the furniture is in. The plan’s job is to make the three evenings possible.

---

## 5. Still — what to send

Photo: a floor plan on the table in the centre, next to a material sample. Your plan, your table.

Caption:
To start a plan we need four things. Ceiling height. A floor plan, or a marked photo. How the room is used. A picture of a light, if you already have one in mind. The picture sizes the piece. The plan places everything else. WhatsApp it to the number in the bio.

## 6. Architect note

Caption:
For a fit-out that is still on drawings: furniture before points, vertical light on the walls you care about, task on its own circuit, concealed light in the cove, the decorative piece anchored to the slab. Wall-wash setback checked on the section, often 600–900 mm at a 3 m ceiling, then adjusted for the optic. Kelvin and CRI are in the other guide. The sequence is here: elorlighting.com/guides/how-a-lighting-plan-is-built
